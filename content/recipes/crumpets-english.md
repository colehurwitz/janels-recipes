2 cups all-purpose flour    1 tablespoon active dried yeast

1 teaspoon salt      1 teaspoon sugar

1 cup warm milk     1 cup warm water

½  teaspoon baking soda

  

Stir the sugar and yeast into the warm milk and let it rest for 10 minutes until frothy. In a large mixing bowl, combine flour and salt.

  

Add the liquid to the flour mixture and beat mixture for about 3 minutes until a thick dough forms. 

Cover the bowl loosely with plastic wrap and set it in a warm place for at least an hour or up to two hours to let the dough rise (it should nearly double in size). This stage is important for the formation of the bubbles that will later form the classic holes in the crumpets.

  

Dissolve the baking soda in the warm water and add it to the dough. If using a stand mixer, beat the batter for a minute or two. If some small lumps remain that is ok. Any remaining lumps will dissolve during the next resting stage.

  

Cover the batter and let it rest in a warm place for at least 30 minutes. After 30 minutes, batter should be nice and bubbly.

  

Prepare the crumpet rights by oiling the inside of each rim. Lightly oil the frying pan. Use a cast iron pan or griddle. Place the rings in the frying pan over medium-high heat.

  

Once the rings are hot, pour the batter into each ring until it is slightly more than half full. Cooking time will vary depending on your stovetop but these will cook for 5-10 minutes. The batter will begin to rise and bubble on top. After several minutes when the tops are cooked and the classic holes are present with the bottoms lightly browned, your crumpets are done. Note: you can either remove the rings and flip the crumpets over to cook the other side for another minute or two until golden or remove them from the rings and toast the crumpets.

  

Serve the crumpets immediately slathered with butter, jam, golden syrup, or honey. To reheat later, toast them in the toaster.
